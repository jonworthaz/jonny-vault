# 01 — The Concept

*The inverted web-design model: distribution first, build last.*

## The market failure we exploit

Millions of small businesses and sole traders — plumbers, cafés, salons,
electricians, garages, groomers — have **no website or a broken one**. Not
because they don't want one, but because:

- They can't picture what they'd get, so the spend feels abstract and risky.
- Agencies quote £1,500–£5,000 up front for something invisible until it's done.
- The buying process is long: brief, mockups, revisions, sign-off. They give up.
- They ignore the cold emails and cold calls that web shops use to reach them.

The value was never the code. It's **closing the imagination gap** and **getting
in front of a buyer who ignores every normal channel.** base-reality is engineered
for exactly those two problems.

## The inversion

| Traditional web dev | base-reality |
|---|---|
| Sell the promise, then build | Build the *preview*, then sell |
| Bespoke from a blank page | Data injected into pre-made templates |
| Reach buyers by email/ads (ignored) | Reach buyers by post (opened) |
| Customer must imagine the result | Customer sees their own site, already built |
| Cost incurred before any yes | Cost incurred (mostly) only after login/yes |

We do the expensive, imaginative part — "here's what your business looks like
online" — **before** asking for money, but we do it *cheaply* because it's
template + data, not bespoke labour.

## The "unbuilt until login" mechanic

This is the core insight and the thing that makes the numbers work.

- We build **20–30 partial templates** once — enough to cover the common local
  trades and a few aesthetics. These are the only real build assets we own.
- We hold a **data record per business** (see [02 — Architecture](./02-architecture.md)):
  logo, name, trade, contact, hours, services, a line or two of profile, brand
  colours lifted from their current presence.
- A business's "website" **does not exist as a built artefact.** It's computed
  on demand: when they log in, the platform selects a template for their trade
  and injects their record, rendering a live, near-finished preview in the browser.
- So we can post **hundreds** of login codes while having built **zero** bespoke
  sites. The ones who never log in cost a stamp. The ones who do get an instant,
  personalised "wow" — and only *those* pull real finishing work from us.

> Partial-but-personalised at scale; finished only on demand. The catalogue is
> fixed cost; the bespoke is pay-on-conversion.

## Why the post is the weapon

- **Signal in a dead channel.** Their inbox is noise; their letterbox is not.
  A well-made card addressed to the business, or hand-dropped at the premises,
  is a pattern-break that gets opened and tried.
- **Curiosity mechanic.** "We built you a website. Log in to see it:
  base-reality.com — code: **XXXX-XXXX**." That is almost impossible to ignore —
  it's about *them*, it's already done, and it costs them nothing to look.
- **Local credibility.** A physical drop signals a real, local operator, not an
  offshore mill. It matches the buyer's mental model of who they'd trust.
- **Measurable.** Each code is unique, so every login is attributable to a
  specific mailer — clean funnel data from a physical channel (see
  [03 — Funnel & Economics](./03-funnel-economics.md)).

## What "finished" means (and when it happens)

The preview is deliberately **90% there** — real content, real branding, a few
pages, mobile-ready — but flagged as a proposal. When they say yes, the
"finishing" is the paid work:

- Their feedback and edits (copy, photos, services, offers).
- Custom domain + real hosting + email.
- Any extra pages, booking/contact integrations, SEO basics.
- Go-live and handover.

Crucially, "finishing" is small and fast *because the hard 90% is already the
template*. That's what lets us quote competitively and deliver in days, not weeks.

## The honesty stance (non-negotiable)

We never imply the site is already live, already public, or the finished
article. The login lands on a clear frame: *"We've mocked up a website for
[Business] from your public information. It's a proposal, not yet live. Like it?
Here's the price to make it real."* This is both right and better-converting —
it removes the "what's the catch" friction. See [04 — Guardrails](./04-guardrails.md).
